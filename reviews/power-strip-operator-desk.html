Power strip that feeds a Windows operator desk

Published 2026-08-31 · 824 Consultants LLC · Problem-first operator notes

This is 120V distribution under a Windows Grok CLI desk. It is not a UPS (wall-side backup), not a dual-GPU PSU (rails inside a chassis), and not a GaN USB-C brick (the laptop PD path). This page is the strip or PDU that has to hold those bricks without melting, daisy-chaining, or pretending USB-A is PD.

The problem

A Grok operator desk is a pile of wall-warts: GaN brick, dock brick, display, powered hub, maybe a small UPS. A travel cube with six outlets on a 2-inch face cannot seat two GaN bricks. The second brick hangs by its prongs. Heat stacks. The strip's "1500W" banner is a laser-printer number, not this load.

Worse: USB-A "charging" ports on the strip that are not PD. Operators plug a phone, then a hub, then wonder why the dock renegotiated. Daisy-chaining strip into UPS into another strip dumps the same sag the UPS was bought to ride through. A strip is not backup. Treating it as a UPS is how scheduled tasks die at 02:00 and the desk claims it is forever-looping.

Verdict criteria (before any SKU link)

  1. Brick spacing — at least two transformer-block outlets that can sit together. If a GaN brick and a dock brick cannot both seat, it fails.
  2. Not a fake UPS — surge protection is optional and listed. Runtime claims, "battery backup" stickers on a strip, or joule marketing that hides missing AVR fail. Backup is the UPS page.
  3. No USB-A pretending to be PD — USB ports on a strip are a trap on this desk. If they exist they must be labeled as phone-only, not a laptop path. A strip that steals the PD brick's job fails.
  4. Cord that reaches the UPS without a second strip — daisy-chain is a fail. Length is a soak, not a spec-sheet 3-foot lie behind the desk.
  5. Listed load at operator draw — size for laptop PD + dock + displays + powered hub. Not a space heater. Not a fake 1500W peak banner on a 14-gauge mystery cord.

Architecture we recommend

  • Wall → UPS → one distribution strip → bricks. Not strip → UPS → strip.
  • GaN brick and dock brick get the spaced outlets. Displays can share closer sockets.
  • Laptop PD stays on the GaN brick or the dock, never on a strip USB port.
  • Heat, NIC, and seat are other pages: cooling stand, USB Ethernet, chair.

Soak test (what "passes" means)

  1. Seat the GaN brick and the dock brick at the same time. If either hangs by its prongs, fail.
  2. Run eight hours. Strip body must not be too hot to hold. One melted face is a fail.
  3. Feed the strip from the UPS. No second strip. A sag that dumps the dock because of a daisy-chain is a fail.
  4. Confirm no device on this desk is taking laptop PD from a USB-A port on the strip.
