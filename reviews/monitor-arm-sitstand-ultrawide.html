Monitor arm that keeps dual ultrawides at eye line on a sit/stand Grok desk

Published 2026-08-31 · 824 Consultants LLC · Problem-first operator notes

This is not the standing desk page and not the dock page. The desk is the keyboard and mouse height. The dock is the video IO. This page is the independent eye-line layer: dual ultrawides that stay at the top-third horizon when the same desktop travels from a sit block to a stand block.

The problem

A 14-hour Windows Grok CLI session on a sit/stand desk is two eye lines, not one. If the panels sit on the desktop, or on a rear riser that travels with the top, standing puts the horizon at sit height. Operators crane, then they stop standing. The desk motors did their job. The screens did not.

Gas-spring dual arms sag by hour eight under two ultrawides. C-clamps walk a particle-board sit/stand top and leave a bite mark. DisplayPort and USB-C that are dressed tight yank the dock when the desk rises, and the dedicated NIC drops with it. A closed-lid laptop on a cooling stand in the well collides with a low-slung arm. Cheap dual-monitor kits sold for 24-inch 1080p panels are not a dual-ultrawide Grok desk.

Verdict criteria (before any SKU link)

  1. Eye line independent of desk travel — sit block and stand block keep the top third of each panel at the same horizon. If the operator must re-pitch after every height change, the arm failed the layer split. The desk moves hands. The arm holds eyes.
  2. Rated for the actual dual ultrawide load — weight and VESA for both panels, not a 24-inch kit with a second clamp. Sag at hour 8, or a panel that droops after a sit→stand cycle, is a fail. One arm per panel is allowed if that is how the load actually holds.
  3. Clamp or grommet that does not crack the top — sit/stand tops are often thin particle board. A C-clamp that walks, splits the edge, or blocks the rear cable tray fails. Grommet through a reinforced hole beats a clamp that eats the laminate.
  4. Slack-looped video and USB-C — DisplayPort, HDMI, and USB-C from the dock must have travel slack for the full desk rise. Tight dress that unplugs the dock or the USB Ethernet dongle on the first stand interval fails this page, not the dock page.
  5. Clearance for closed-lid laptop + cooling stand — the well in front of the panels is for a closed-lid Windows Grok host on a stand. An arm crossbar that hits the lid, blocks intake, or forces the laptop onto the keyboard plane fails.

Architecture we recommend

  • Desk first for keyboard height. Arm second for eyes. Dock third for outputs. Do not buy a “standing desk with monitors included” kit and call the layer split done.
  • Route DisplayPort and USB-C with a slack loop at the hinge, then into the arm’s cable channel. The loop must survive the full sit→stand travel.
  • Keep the laptop well in front of the panels. The cooling stand is not a monitor riser.
  • Other layers stay on their own pages: standing desk, dock, cooling stand, chair.

Soak test (what "passes" means)

  1. Sit two hours at the real Grok CLI height. Horizon stays in the top third. If the neck cranes down into the panel, fail.
  2. Raise the desk to stand without touching the arm. Horizon must still be the top third. A re-pitch after every rise is a fail.
  3. Repeat sit → stand → sit ten times. Measure sag at the bottom bezel. Visible droop or a clamp that walked is a fail.
  4. Confirm DisplayPort and the dedicated NIC are still seated after the last rise. A yanked dock is a fail on this page.
  5. Confirm the closed-lid laptop on the cooling stand still has intake clearance. A crossbar on the lid is a fail.
