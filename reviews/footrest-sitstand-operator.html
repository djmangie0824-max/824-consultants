Footrest that survives sit blocks on a sit/stand Grok desk

Published 2026-08-31 · 824 Consultants LLC · Problem-first operator notes

This is not the chair page and not the standing desk page. The chair is the sit-block body. The desk is the height layer. This page is the feet layer that the chair cannot fix: dangling calves at sitting height, then a trip hazard when the same well becomes a stand block.

The problem

A 14-hour Windows Grok CLI session on a sit/stand desk is not one posture. Sit blocks last hours. If the chair is set so the keyboard is level, feet often hang. Lower-back pain that operators blame on the racing throne is actually a missing foot plane. Inflatable travel pillows collapse by hour three. Under-desk drawers become a footrest until they dump a brick onto the power strip.

Worse: the same object stays in the well when the desk goes to stand. A six-inch foam wedge that saved the sit block is a shin-breaker at 42 inches of desktop. Operators kick it under the UPS, then the UPS vents into carpet. A footrest that only works in one posture fails this desk.

Verdict criteria (before any SKU link)

  1. Sit-block height that lets both feet rest flat — calves not dangling, heels not jammed. If the chair must drop so low the desk edge hits the thighs, the footrest failed the layer split.
  2. Not a trip in the stand block — it must park, fold, or kick aside without becoming a shin-breaker under a raised desktop. A one-posture brick fails.
  3. Does not steal the UPS / strip well — wall → UPS → strip lives under this desk. A footrest that sits on the strip or blocks UPS vents fails. See UPS and strip.
  4. Does not collapse at hour 6 — inflatable, memory-foam puck, or stacked books that slide are a fail. Hard enough to be a plane, not a cushion that becomes a slope.
  5. Width for both feet — a single-foot bar or a rocking pedal that wants a gym is a fail on a CLI desk.

Architecture we recommend

  • Chair first, then feet, then desk height. Do not drop the chair to fake a foot plane.
  • Keep the under-desk well for UPS + strip. Foot plane is in front of the well, not on top of it.
  • Stand intervals should not require a scavenger hunt. Park or fold in one motion.
  • Seat and height are other pages: chair, standing desk.

Soak test (what "passes" means)

  1. Sit two hours at the real Grok CLI height. Heels stay planted. If either foot goes numb or dangles, fail.
  2. Raise the desk to stand. Kick or fold the rest in one motion. A shin hit or a dumped UPS is a fail.
  3. Confirm the strip and UPS still have clearance and vents. A rest sitting on a brick is a fail.
  4. Repeat sit → stand → sit four times in a day. If you stop using it, it failed comfort, not a photo.
